# ProteinMisfolding

## Overview
ProteinMisfolding is a software that models protein misfolding, using specifically the molecular and sequence-level properties of amyloid-β. Misfolded proteins often 'form large polymers of unwanted aggregates' (Ashraf et al., 2014); this occurrence is prevalent in neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's disease (AD). Amyloid deposits are formed when an α-helix transforms into a β-helix; AD is 'characterised by the accumulation of amyloid-β in the brain' (Ashraf et al., 2014).

Amyloid precursor protein (APP) is a single-pass type I transmembrane glycoprotein; synthesised in the endoplasmic reticulum, it possesses a significant role in neuronal development, particularly during maturation and neural growth (Bamford et al., 2020), although its physiological functions are unclear. APP is a protein of up to 770 amino acids, comprising a cytoplasmic tail, a transmembrane segment with an α-helical structure and an extracellular domain (sAPP) (Gralle and Ferreira, 2007).
